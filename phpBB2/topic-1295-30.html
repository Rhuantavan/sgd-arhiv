Citiram:

Another thing I've played around with a bit lately is XNA, Microsoft's mysterious gamedev system/environment. I'm still not 100% sure what XNA's all about, but as far as I can tell it appears to be some kind of DX-like wrapper around, erm, DX.

A lot of the concepts in XNA are similar to DX, only a tad higher level. You still need to get your head around matrices and shaders in a big way and there is no 'scene graph' aspect to it at all (I think) so it's all kind of different from where I wanna go with 3D.

And of course, it's all based around C#, which I assumes rules out linking with c/c++ libraries - certainly on the xbox360 anyway. I actually think this is a bit of a big deal, as it means no ODE, OGG, or anything like that! Still, it's kind of understandable and possibly a good thing as it keeps things much simpler.

It also has a strangely oh-so-familiar MS feel to it - a kind of contrived simplicity, like there's a lot going on behind the scenes that MS don't want you to know about, you certainly don't want to know about, but you just know you'll eventually have to find out! I could be wrong here - perhaps it's just a side effect of modern hyper-automated IDE's, I dunno...

I *did* consider doing something in XNA for a little holiday project - but think I'll stick with Max instead! So if anyone's interesting in knocking up some simple, low poly 'defender' models, please get in touch...

I guess that, for me anyway, although the idea of getting something running on a console rocks, getting something running on Win32, MacOS and Linux still rocks harder! Also, Windows XNA games are gonna be burdened but not only the .NET framework (which I guess many people have) but also the XNA framework (which is probably not so common).

But I think, all in all, Microsoft should really be applauded for at least exploring the idea of hobbyist game development on a console in this way...although I would really much rather someone just came out with a Linux based console!
